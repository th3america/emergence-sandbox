## Central question

**What can we make it do?**

The assembly space accepts concepts, lenses, methods, key context, rules, memories, capabilities and previous creations as functional pieces. Jason can supply an arrangement in one instruction. Synthesis resolves a configuration, design, method or artifact; functional testing evaluates its claims separately.

## Representation and interaction

Each piece carries id, type, name, function, scope, actual content, influences, dependencies and provenance. Relationships describe how one piece frames, bounds, informs, enables, transforms or constrains another. Preserve source meaning and mark conflicts rather than silently substituting definitions.

An assembly records the goal, selected pieces and versions, relationships, configuration, starting state, environment and available operations. Context establishes which facts matter to this goal; a lens changes attention and interpretation. A method transforms the configured inputs. Conceptual pieces need not be executable code.

The synthesis result contains:

1. Assembly map: how each piece contributed, relationships, conflicts and dependencies.
2. Resolved creation: the actual design, method, configuration or artifact, labeled by the kind of result created.
3. Testable claims: expected behavior, fixtures or observations needed, and scope.
4. Provenance and lineage: source pieces, participant, subsequent revisions and actual reuse.

Successful results may become compound pieces. Failures may yield attributed dependency findings. Neither availability nor a saved record alone proves subsequent use.

## AGI principles review

The design used Jason's separately preserved July 18, 2026 Principles of AGI source. That private source is not included in this public staging copy. The terms below are the project's attributed local definitions and do not silently substitute the conventional expansion of AGI.

```text
AGI is Artifically Generated Intelligence.
it generates (created) methods of using data to achieve goals.
intelligence is the use of data to achieve goals
smart is how well intelligence is use.
```

| Source principles | Planned application | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4: generated intelligence, data-to-goal use, quality, method generation | Resolve a method or creation from arranged pieces; evaluate quality separately from existence | Original goal, source data, proposed method, artifact, observed outcome and quality criteria |
| 5: self-prompting + self-governance | Allow intermediate demands and governed correction within the authorized assembly | Supplied instructions versus participant-generated demands; selection/revision/stop decisions |
| 6–7: pulse supports recurrence but is not self-prompting | Represent continuation triggers separately from generated questions; one-shot synthesis may contain bounded internal iterations | Trigger origin, iteration and reason to continue; no automatic scheduler added by this plan |
| 8: self-prompting supplies motion | A missing function can lead to a question, subgoal or proposed new piece without Jason specifying each step | Attributed intermediate demand and its connection to the parent goal |
| 9: self-governance supplies bounds and correction | Detect conflicting pieces, revise arrangements, reject unsuitable routes, stop when appropriate | Conflict, applied rule, decision and resulting change |
| 10: scoped continuation | Keep internal recursion within the selected task and available operations | Scope, continuation boundaries and completion reason |
| 11: capability versus claim | Separate proposed, constructed and tested results | Execution/source evidence; no inference of general ability from a fixture pass |
| 12–14: AGI, sovereignty and leased authority differ | Store authority separately from piece capability or behavioral performance | Actual permissions and operator scope; no authority granted by a concept block |
| 15: motion/control before later capability strata | Make goal generation and governance visible across the assembly before expanding tools | Trace of demand, method selection, result review and correction |

Tool creation is one form of method generation. A generated tool must carry its input/output contract and observed test scope before being reused operationally. The principles work across the configuration; this is not a requirement for one module per principle.

## Acceptance examples

- Lens + method + key context produces an assembly map and a concrete result, not just a list of capabilities.
- The same pieces arranged differently can produce different configurations; the UI exposes the changed relationship.
- A conceptual design can be saved as a creation without being mislabeled tested software.
- Missing-function identification records whether Jason supplied it or the participant generated it.
- A conflicting rule/context combination produces a visible conflict and an attributed resolution.
- A created piece reused later yields a source-result → piece → consumer lineage backed by the actual assembly or execution.
- A countercheck can disprove a functional claim without erasing the fact that an artifact was created.
